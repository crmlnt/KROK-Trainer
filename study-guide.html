Chapter 01

Understanding KROK 1

Start with the examination, not the question bank

KROK 1 is a component of Ukraine’s Unified State Qualification Exam (USQE) assessing foundational medical sciences. Preparing well means understanding mechanisms and relationships—not simply remembering answer positions.

150Multiple-choice questions
1Correct answer per question
3 hoursListed for foreign students

The official Testing Board describes a 2-hour-30-minute duration for Ukrainian students and 3 hours for foreign students. Its English information page specifies a 64% passing threshold for 2025; do not assume that figure is automatically confirmed for every later sitting. Always consult your university and the current official instructions for your examination date.

A content structure applicable from 2026 has been published. Use that official blueprint to check your coverage, rather than assuming the subjects or proportions in historical booklets match the current examination.

Build a concept map

Connect anatomy to physiology, physiology to pathology, and pathology to pharmacology. For example, understanding cardiac conduction helps with normal physiology, ECG interpretation, and the effects of antiarrhythmic drugs.

Chapter 02

Are Historical KROK Booklets Still Useful?

Use historical questions critically

Historical KROK booklets remain valuable for retrieval practice, recognizing common question formats, and identifying gaps in foundational knowledge. They are a practice resource, not a substitute for the current official syllabus.

What they can help you do

  • Practice recalling information without looking at notes.
  • Recognize how basic-science concepts may be tested in multiple-choice format.
  • Identify recurring weaknesses and unfamiliar English terminology.
  • Review explanations and transfer knowledge to new examples.

What they cannot guarantee

Historical questions do not establish the content, difficulty, or passing probability of a future examination. Some may include transcription errors, outdated terminology, ambiguous wording, or disputed answer keys. A high score on familiar items may partly reflect question recognition rather than transferable understanding.

Evidence boundary

Any preliminary comparison between a historical database and a single newer exam variant is exploratory. We do not publish an estimated percentage of current-exam coverage or imply that the bank predicts the official examination.

A useful question-review habit

After every answer, explain why the selected option is correct and why at least one plausible distractor is wrong. If the key conflicts with reliable sources, flag the item rather than memorizing a potentially incorrect explanation.

Chapter 03

The Core Principle of Effective KROK Preparation

Practice → Identify gaps → Understand → Review → Retest

Progress is more meaningful when each question leads to a learning decision. Use this repeatable five-stage cycle rather than treating question count as the main objective.

01PracticeAttempt questions independently, without immediate reference to notes.
02Identify gapsSeparate unfamiliar facts from misunderstandings and reading errors.
03UnderstandConsult reliable materials and explain the mechanism in your own words.
04ReviewRevisit difficult concepts after a delay, not only immediately.
05RetestTry varied questions to check whether the knowledge transfers.

A correct answer may still deserve review if it was a guess. An incorrect answer is useful when it exposes a specific, fixable weakness.

Ask three questions after a mistake

  • What concept was being tested?
  • Why did my reasoning fail?
  • How will I check this concept again later?

Chapter 04

Evidence-Based Study Methods

Learn efficiently, not just intensively

Retrieval practice

Try to recall information before checking the answer. Practice testing has substantial evidence for improving retention compared with relying only on passive review. [1, 2]

Distributed practice and spaced review

Spread review over time instead of concentrating all repetition in one session. For example, you might revisit a difficult concept on days 1, 3, 7, 14, and 30 after initial study. These intervals are illustrative, not a universally validated schedule. [1, 3]

Interleaving

Once core concepts are reasonably secure, mix related disciplines and problem types. Interleaving can help learners distinguish similar concepts, but the evidence base is more context-dependent than that for retrieval and distributed practice. [1, 4]

Classify your errors

Knowledge gapConceptual misunderstandingSimilar-concept confusionLanguage difficultyAttention or reasoning error

Different errors need different remedies. A language difficulty may require defining a term; a conceptual misunderstanding requires rebuilding the mechanism.

Two realistic routines

90-minute study block

10 min recall · 35 min questions · 30 min error analysis · 15 min delayed-review planning

30-minute study block

5 min recall · 15 min focused questions · 10 min explanation and notes

Avoid relying on rereading, highlighting alone, memorizing answer positions, or completing many questions without reviewing mistakes.

Chapter 05

Choose Your KROK 1 Study Plan

A schedule should adapt to your progress

Begin with a mixed-subject diagnostic session of approximately 50–100 questions if your current knowledge allows it. Treat the result as an initial signal, not a definitive readiness score. The schedules below are illustrative planning frameworks, not scientifically validated prescriptions or replicas of the official 2026 subject weighting.

FOUNDATION FIRST

12 weeks

Approximately 1–2 hours per day

  1. Weeks 1–4: physiology, biochemistry, anatomy and histology, pathophysiology.
  2. Weeks 5–8: pharmacology, microbiology, pathological anatomy, integration and weaker topics.
  3. Weeks 9–12: mixed practice, timed sessions, error review, simulations and consolidation.

Illustrative target: 20–40 questions/day.

BALANCED REVIEW

8 weeks

Approximately 2–3 hours per day

  1. Weeks 1–2: physiology, biochemistry, anatomy and histology.
  2. Weeks 3–4: pathophysiology, pathological anatomy and pharmacology.
  3. Weeks 5–6: microbiology, weaker subjects and increasingly mixed practice.
  4. Weeks 7–8: simulations, targeted correction and final review.

Illustrative target: 30–60 questions/day.

INTENSIVE CONSOLIDATION

4 weeks

Approximately 3–4 hours per day

  1. Week 1: diagnostic assessment and high-priority core review.
  2. Week 2: intensive mixed questions with systematic error analysis.
  3. Week 3: realistic simulations and focused weak-area review.
  4. Week 4: consolidation, lighter revision and exam preparation.

Illustrative target: 50–80 questions/day. Best suited to students with an existing foundation.

Adjust every week

Increase attention to persistently weak subjects, retain periodic review of strong areas, and reduce question volume when meaningful review becomes impossible. Prioritize the current official content structure over any illustrative subject order.

Chapter 06

Using KROK Trainer Effectively

Turn features into a learning workflow

KROK Trainer can support a structured cycle of question practice, explanation, assessment, and reflection. It does not replace textbooks, the official syllabus, or authoritative medical references.

Practice Mode

Choose a subject and work through questions with feedback. Answer independently, then investigate the reasoning. This mode is particularly useful for initial learning and targeted revision.

AI Explain

Use AI-generated explanations to explore mechanisms and distinguish answer options. Explanations may be incomplete or incorrect: verify unfamiliar or clinically important claims using reliable sources. AI Explain is a study aid, not an official answer key.

Exam Mode

Complete question sets without immediate feedback and review your results afterward. Use mixed-subject sessions progressively as your preparation advances. A score is informative but cannot guarantee official-exam performance.

Exam History and mistake review

Registered users can revisit saved examination sessions and inspect individual responses when question-level records are available. Some older sessions do not have detailed saved answers. A session-level retry of incorrect answers is available; it is not the same as a persistent, cross-session wrong-question filter.

Statistics and Weak Subjects

Review patterns across eligible Exam Mode sessions, consider the sample size behind each percentage, and use weak-subject signals to adjust study time. Do not interpret a small number of questions as a stable measure of competence.

My Notes

Registered users can attach concise notes to questions in Practice Mode. Summarize mechanisms in your own words, then try to recall the explanation before rereading your note.

Chapter 07

Final Exam Preparation

Prepare for the examination, not just another quiz

The final two weeks

Consolidate important concepts, revisit persistent weaknesses, and complete mixed-subject simulations. Avoid restarting every textbook or practicing only your strongest subjects. Review simulations rather than collecting scores.

The last 72 hours

72–48hTargeted revisionReview frequently missed mechanisms and concise notes.
48–24hReduce overloadUse light practice if helpful; confirm examination logistics.
Final 24hProtect restPrepare documents, eat regularly, hydrate, and prioritize sleep.

Simulate realistically

Use mixed questions, avoid external help, and apply a time limit based on current official instructions. The Testing Board's published duration differs for Ukrainian and foreign students; verify the rules applicable to your sitting. Simulation scores are not guarantees of success.

Manage your time

Average time per question = total examination time ÷ number of questions. This is a planning aid, not a rigid limit. Read carefully, identify discriminating clues, eliminate clearly inconsistent options, decide, and move on. Return to difficult questions only if the examination interface permits it.

When you do not know an answer

Identify the discipline, recall the underlying mechanism, look for clues, and eliminate implausible choices. Avoid inventing unsupported explanations or allowing one question to consume time needed for the rest.

Sleep and examination readiness

Sleep supports memory and attention; insufficient sleep can impair cognitive performance. Maintain a reasonably consistent schedule, avoid unusually late caffeine intake, and do not sacrifice rest for another large practice set. [5, 6]

Examination-day checklist

  • Verify official instructions, identity documents and arrival details.
  • Confirm permitted materials, navigation rules and how answers are recorded.
  • Read questions carefully and pace yourself.
  • Change an answer when you identify a clear reasoning error—not because of superstition.
  • Follow official result and appeal channels afterward.

Learn the principles. Practice applying them. Review what you do not understand.
